Method: linear regression on the three most recent years, which captures the post-peak phase. Negative values are clipped to zero. Limitations: very few points, 2021 treated as a complete year, and no allowance for licensing, competition or production cycles. Read it as an indicative trend, not an exact figure.

Insights

What the analysis shows

Findings

  • Movie-dominant, recent-heavy, US-centred catalogue.
  • Release years span 1925–2021, most after 2010, peaking 2017–2018.
  • Movie length is near 100 min; most shows have 1–2 seasons.

Predictions

  • Mix: stays movie-heavy with short-season series.
  • Length: new movies stay at or just under 100 min.
  • Geography: India, South Korea and Japan gain share.

Feature engineering
df["date_added"] = pd.to_datetime(
    df["date_added"].str.strip(), errors="coerce")
df["added_year"] = df["date_added"].dt.year
movies = df[df["type"] == "Movie"]
shows = df[df["type"] == "TV Show"]
df["movie_duration_min"] = movies["duration"].str.extract(r"(\d+)").astype(float)
df["tv_seasons"] = shows["duration"].str.extract(r"(\d+)").astype(float)
IQR outlier detection
Q1 = df["release_year"].quantile(0.25)
Q3 = df["release_year"].quantile(0.75)
IQR = Q3 - Q1
lower, upper = Q1 - 1.5 * IQR, Q3 + 1.5 * IQR
outliers = df[(df["release_year"] < lower) | (df["release_year"] > upper)]
